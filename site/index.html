Tyr Tank Atlas

Every tank in Tyr placed against every other, from the game's own data tables. Dots are tanks. Colour is class. Hollow dots are tanks the files mark as work in progress, which look unreleased. Pick a tank to follow it through every chart.

Where each tank sits

Each row is one stat, and each dot is one tank placed along it. The better end of every row is on the right, so stats where lower is better (reload, dispersion, cost) run reversed. The tank at the far right is named.

How each stat is spread

The smooth curve is a kernel density estimate of the 18 tanks: where the curve is high, many tanks sit close together. The dashed curve is a normal distribution with the same mean and spread, so you can see where the real roster bunches up or leaves gaps. Below, the same values split by class, with each class mean marked.

One tank against the roster

Each bar is how many standard deviations the tank sits from the roster mean on that stat. Bars to the right are better, whichever way the stat runs, so a fast reload points right. Bars past ±2 are unusual for this roster.

Which stats move together

Pearson correlation of base stats across the 18 tanks. Blue means two stats rise together, red means one rises as the other falls, grey means no straight-line link. Rows are clustered so stats that move together sit next to each other. With 18 tanks, anything under about ±0.47 could be chance.

The bottom block takes the game's own designer ratings (firepower, durability and so on, shown in the tank picker) and correlates each with the real stats, in the same column order. Correlations use raw values, so a positive link with reload means longer reloads.

    Any stat against any stat

    The line is the Pareto frontier for the two stats you pick: the tanks no other tank beats on both at once. Anything below the line has a strictly better option on both counts, unless its ability makes up the gap.

    The axes are plain values, not reversed. Stats where lower is better (reload, cost, dispersion) have their frontier on the low side.

    The tank map

    Every base stat squeezed into two dimensions with principal component analysis. Tanks close together play alike on paper. Arrows show which way each stat pulls; longer arrows explain more of the spread.

    How alike they are

    Distance between every pair of tanks across all base stats, scaled so each stat counts equally. Darker means more alike. Rows are grouped so families sit together.

      What 31 talent points buy

      Base value to the best value the talent tree allows for that stat. On every tank but Fuse, 31 points max damage, reload, health and speed at the same time, so the core build has no trade-off.

      Components: the reference fight

      Each tank takes three of 43 components. To compare them, every tank gets its 31-point core talent build and fights a 60 second one on one against a median tank. The duel score is effective damage per second times effective health, and gain is how much a component or loadout raises it over no components. Situational effects only count in scenarios where their trigger happens.

      What each component is worth on each tank

      Duel gain from one component on its own. Columns are grouped by the component's category. A ringed cell is an outlier: that component is worth far more on that tank than on the typical tank. Empty columns are components the duel score cannot see on their own: speed, camouflage, aim, module damage, or energy that needs a partner component to spend it.

      Every loadout for one tank

      All 12,341 three-component loadouts placed by the damage and health they give. Darker cells hold more loadouts. The dots are the Pareto front: loadouts no other loadout beats on damage, health and speed at once. The line joins the ones that win on damage and health alone. The faint curve is equal duel score through the best loadout.

      How much the loadout matters

      For each tank, how the duel gain of all 12,341 loadouts is spread. A wide ridge means picking well matters a lot on that tank; a narrow one means most loadouts end up close. Ticks mark the median and the 90th percentile. Heights are square-root scaled so the long tails stay visible.
