      Which stats move together

      Pearson correlation of base stats across the 18 tanks. Blue means two stats rise together, red means one rises as the other falls, grey means no straight-line link.         Components: the reference fight

        Each tank takes three of 43 components. To compare them, every tank gets its 31-point core talent build and fights a 60 second one on one against a median tank. Situational effects only count in scenarios where their trigger happens.

        Armor

        How the armor holds up

        Armor read from the game's per-triangle armor meshes and tested with the game's own penetration rule.

        Armor from every side

        Armor comes from the game's own armor meshes: every triangle carries a thickness in mm, and every shot is decided by the game's rule. Over 80° from the surface always bounces, a gun with four times the plate's thickness goes through at any angle, otherwise thickness ÷ cos(angle) against penetration, with a 50/50 band up to 1.25× penetration. Hits on treads only track you and do no HP damage. Each chart casts a grid of parallel shots every 6 cm across the tank and counts what happens, assuming hits land evenly over the visible silhouette.

        Armor shape of every tank

        Each rose shows, for level shots from every direction, the share of hits that do no damage. Bigger means tougher from that side. Sorted from toughest all-round.

        Breakpoints

        A breakpoint is where a small change moves a matchup by a whole shot. These lists use the selected tank: which components or ammo cut a shot off its kills, and which enemies need an extra hit if it runs Bulkheads or Mobility Surger.

        Aiming for weak spots and crits

        Good players aim at weak spots and crit modules at close and mid range. For the selected tank as the target, this is the expected damage chance when aiming at its best spot, against how far your shots land from where you aim (1σ). Misses off the tank count as no damage. Crit modules: a bonus spot adds 50% damage, fire burns 20 per second for 8 s, engine halves speed for 8 s.

        Maps

        Where to stand

        Line of sight over every map, built from the game's terrain and every placed object. For each spot a tank could sit, we sweep sight lines out to 400 m and measure how much enemy tank it can see, and how much of it can be seen.

        Sight lines

        Blue spots see more than they are seen: crests you can fight over hull-down, bush edges, and positions overlooking open ground. Red spots are the opposite. Numbered spots are the strongest open-ground positions that also see a lot of the map; B spots are bushes with the best views, which stay hidden from anyone beyond 50 m (in this model, at least until you fire). Coverage shows every spot that can see a capture zone's centre with at least a quarter of a tank exposed.

        How it works: 2 m cells; your gun at 2.5 m above the ground; an enemy counts as a 2.5 m column, and each sight line credits the share of that column above the highest obstacle, so a tank behind a crest counts as partly seen. Bushes block sight like a 3 m wall and hide a tank inside them beyond 50 m. The playable area is estimated from where the zones, spawns and bushes are and from slopes a tank can climb; the game's invisible walls are not in the files we read. Camouflage and view range differences between tanks are not applied yet.

        Method

        Where the numbers come from

        This is an unofficial fan analysis. It is not made or checked by the game's developers, and the numbers can be wrong or out of date after a patch.

        Sources

        Everything is read from the game's own files, not measured in matches. The current data comes from .

        • Tank stats come from the game's data tables: health, gun, reload, dispersion, mobility, camouflage, ability numbers and the designer ratings shown in the in-game tank picker. Damage per second is shell damage times clip size over the time to fire and reload one clip.
        • Talent trees come from the same file the game client downloads from the game's CDN (). The 31-point core build is the talent build that raises damage per second, shell damage, health and speed as far as 31 points allow. On every tank but Fuse those four max out together; on Fuse damage per second comes first.
        • Components and their effects come from the game's component data. The game's descriptions carry placeholders instead of level values, so the site shows N where a number would go.
        • Armor comes from each tank's armor mesh: every triangle carries a thickness in mm, and a second texture marks treads and crit modules.

        The penetration rule

        • A hit more than 80° from the surface normal always bounces.
        • A gun with at least four times the plate's thickness in penetration goes through at any angle.
        • Otherwise the effective armor is thickness ÷ cos(angle), compared with the gun's penetration.
        • Between the gun's penetration and 1.25 times it, a hit is a 50/50. Which side of the gun's number the band sits on is our reading of the game's settings.
        • Hits on treads track the tank and do no HP damage.

        The armor charts cast a grid of parallel shots every 6 cm across the tank from 72 directions around it and 8 heights above it, and count what happens. They assume hits land evenly over the visible silhouette, so they ignore aiming at weak spots.

        Face-offs

        Each face-off puts two tanks on their 31-point core builds front to front (or both angled 30°). Time to kill comes from damage per second, health, and each gun's chance to do damage against the other's armor from that angle. It ignores aiming at weak spots, abilities, components and ammo.

        The component reference fight

        Components are scored in a 60 second one on one against a median tank, with the tank on its 31-point core build. The duel score is effective damage per second times effective health. Situational effects only count in scenarios where their trigger happens.

          What it leaves out

          • Player skill, maps, teams, spotting and aiming at weak spots.
          • Ammo types other than standard shells in the stat charts.
          • Abilities, except where their numbers appear as stats or a component pays out on them.
          • Tanks the files mark as work in progress may change or never ship.
